# Writing reviews mark with codes and never give the fix

Written corrective feedback improves accuracy (Kang and Han 2015), and focused feedback is easier to act on than correcting everything (Ellis 2008). Whether to give the correct form is contested: direct and indirect feedback both worked, with direct stronger on grammar (Van Beuningen 2012; Bitchener and Knoch 2010). lingo chooses indirect, coded marking with a hint, at most eight marks a round, because a review that writes the fix becomes the corrected text the AI rules forbid, and the learner then has nothing left to do.

## Considered options

- **Direct correction.** Rejected as a design choice, not on the evidence: it is the AI writing the learner's text.
- **Direct correction for grammar at low levels only.** Partly taken: at A0 and A1 the hint may name the rule, which is as close to direct as lingo goes.

## Consequences

- Rounds: the learner fixes, reruns, and the review carries marks over as fixed or open.
- The review eval checks that neither planted fix appears.
